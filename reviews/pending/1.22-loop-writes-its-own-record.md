## WHAT THIS CARD PROPOSES

A tool that reads advisor rounds out of `deliberation_rounds` and writes them
into `knowledge_messages` under a source of their own, so the exchanges are
retrievable by the same queries that reach every other corpus. Invoked from
closeout beside the ingest tools already there.

**Four decisions this card must make, and I want your answer on each rather than
my own:**

1. **Granularity.** One knowledge row per advisor round, or one per objection
   inside a round? A round is up to 4,000 characters of reasoning covering
   several findings. Rows are what search returns, so this decides what a future
   reviewer gets back.

2. **What accompanies the text.** The objection alone is not self-describing.
   Candidates: the card id, the round number, the lineage, the frame verdict, the
   result verdict, the packet hash. Which of these must travel with the text for
   it to be useful six weeks from now, and which are noise.

3. **Idempotence.** Closeout runs repeatedly. The tool must not duplicate rows
   already written. `source_key` is the natural identity — is
   `advisor/<card>/<lineage>/<round>` sufficient, and what happens when a row is
   corrected after ingest.

4. **When it writes.** At closeout, beside the existing tools — or at the moment
   the round is recorded. Item 1.26 argues the closeout-only pattern means every
   reader between two closeouts sees stale state. Writing at round time is
   barely more code. **Is there a reason to prefer closeout that I am not
   seeing?**

## THE HALF THIS CARD DOES NOT DO, NAMED SO IT IS NOT MISTAKEN FOR COMPLETE

**Capture is not retrieval.** Writing the exchanges into the knowledge base does
not make you warm. You have no tools. You cannot query the knowledge base, and
nothing in this card changes that. The record would exist and remain unreachable
to the party that produced it.

**What would actually end the cold start is the packet carrying it** — the
assembly step putting prior relevant exchanges in front of you before you answer.
That is a second piece of work, it is not in this card, and without it this card
produces a write-only archive.

**Say whether that makes this card worth doing on its own.** The argument for is
that an archive nobody can yet read is still better than an exchange that no
longer exists, and the retrieval step cannot be built against nothing. The
argument against is that it is infrastructure with no consumer, which is the
defect item 2.37 names. **I do not think this is obvious and I would rather you
decide it than me.**
