# THE CARD THAT WAS APPROVED — close the gap that put item selection back on Eric

## What was asked for

Eric asked to stop being the person who carries information between tools. When
card-writing moved to Claude Code, nothing was left to propose what to work on
next, so choosing the next item landed back on him — the exact role the advisor
system exists to remove him from.

## What this card set out to do

1. Make the reviewers always ask "is this the right work?" before reviewing the
   design, in every round, so a wrong direction is caught before effort is spent
   rather than after. Across eight prior reviews, nobody had ever questioned
   direction — it had never been asked for.
2. Make the reviewers propose what to do next when work finishes, so Eric picks
   from a short list instead of deriving one from a 120-item document he does
   not read.
3. Record which item is being worked, so that list does not propose finished
   work.

## What it deliberately did not do

Nothing advances the current-item record by itself. Nothing forces the proposal
step to consult it — that is a behavioural property of a model reading prose,
not something this card can guarantee.
